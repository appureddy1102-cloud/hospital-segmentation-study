> **No real hospital data was supplied with this project brief.** Every record in this
> repository is generated by [`src/generate_synthetic_data.py`](src/generate_synthetic_data.py)
> from a fixed seed (`20241111`) and is **fully reproducible**.
>
> **Nothing here describes a real hospital, real patients or real revenue.** The methods,
> statistics, code and decision logic are production-grade and transfer unchanged to a real
> extract — only the input file paths change.
>
> The generator is calibrated to realistic Indian community-hospital operating parameters
> (OPD footfall, ALOS, tariff bands, GST treatment of healthcare services) and deliberately
> injects **seven families of data-quality defects** so that the cleaning stage in this
> project is a genuine analytical exercise rather than a narrative. A defect register is
> written alongside the data, which lets the cleaning pipeline's detection rate be validated
> against ground truth rather than asserted.

## What this project does

A four-hospital community network had 36 months of OPD, inpatient, laboratory and billing
records and no patient-level view of its own panel. This project:

1. **Cleans** ~500k transactional records across 5 tables, remediating 13 defect classes with
   a recorded rule for each — and deletes nothing without a reason code.
2. **Engineers** 21 patient-level features covering recency, frequency, monetary value and
   15 behavioural/clinical dimensions.
3. **Segments the panel two independent ways** — quantile RFM scoring and K-means clustering
   — and compares them on ten criteria.
4. **Selects** the operational segmentation on a composite utility score, explicitly *not* on
   silhouette alone, and documents why k=2 was rejected despite winning on separation.
5. **Profiles** each segment on 19 KPIs and verifies numerically that the differences are real.
6. **Tests stability** by re-fitting the model independently on two 18-month periods and
   matching clusters by profile with the Hungarian algorithm.
7. **Costs four interventions** with a transparent, sensitivity-tested financial model.

## Method notes worth reading

Three decisions in this project are non-obvious and are documented in full rather than
buried in code:

**1. `visits_per_month` had to be redefined.** The intuitive definition — `frequency ÷
tenure_months` — gives a single-visit patient a tenure of exactly one month, hence the
*maximum possible* rate for the *least engaged* patient. The correlation heatmap caught it
(ρ = −0.75 against frequency instead of the expected strong positive). It was replaced with a
fixed-window rate and the tenure-based version retained only as a diagnostic.
See [`docs/METHODOLOGY.md`](docs/METHODOLOGY.md#2-the-feature-that-had-to-be-redefined).

**2. k=3 was selected despite k=2 winning on silhouette.** k=2 splits the panel into engaged
and disengaged — geometrically clean, operationally useless, because 64% of the panel lands
in one residual. Selection uses a composite operational utility score weighted on KPI spread,
segment-size balance, silhouette and revenue separation, and disqualifies any solution whose
smallest segment falls below the 5% viability floor. k=5 through k=10 all fall below it.
See [`docs/METHODOLOGY.md`](docs/METHODOLOGY.md#3-why-k3-and-not-the-highest-silhouette).

**3. The GST correction was reconstructed, not assumed.** A blanket "divide by 1.05" would
have corrupted 96% of billing rows. The pipeline establishes the identity empirically
(`total = base + gst` holds for 96.0% of rows; the residual 1.2% satisfy
`total = base + 2 × gst`), inspects the rate by service type (0% healthcare services, 5%
pharmacy, 12% consumables), and rebuilds `total` from `base` and the row's own rate.
See [`docs/METHODOLOGY.md`](docs/METHODOLOGY.md#1-the-gst-investigation).

## Ethical constraints

This is a healthcare setting. The segmentation describes **engagement and resource intensity**
— it must never be used to deny, delay or reduce medically necessary treatment on the basis of
a patient's financial value. The recommendations are deliberately framed so that the
*lowest-value* segment (54% of the panel, 3% of revenue) receives the largest proportional
investment in early detection, not the least. Any operational deployment should pass clinical
governance review before go-live.

All identifiers are synthetic surrogates. No real personal data is present anywhere in this
repository. See [`docs/DATA_PROVENANCE.md`](docs/DATA_PROVENANCE.md).

## Limitations (abbreviated)

- All data is synthetic — figures are not hospital facts.
- Gross billing is observed; **cost is not**. Margin rates are declared assumptions.
- Benefit figures are annualised projections from cross-sectional unit economics, not measured
  treatment effects from a controlled pilot.
- K-means assumes convex, similarly-sized clusters; the data is more of a continuum
  (silhouette 0.43–0.51 is honest but modest).
- Segmentation is only moderately stable over 18 months (ARI 0.158) — monthly re-scoring is
  required, and that instability is itself reported as a finding.
- No clinical severity adjustment (SOFA/Charlson); "high-acuity" is high-acuity by resource
  consumption, which is a proxy.

Full limitations in section 18 of the Word report.
